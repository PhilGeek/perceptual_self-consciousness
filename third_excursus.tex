\chapter{Third Excursus} % (fold)
\label{cha:third_excursus}

\section{Aspects of Perception} % (fold)
\label{sec:procession_and_reversion_as_aspects_of_perception}

Our scribe, having worked late copying, hurries down the hall, only to catch a glimpse of Proclus, through an open door, performing this evening's theurgic rites. In seeing Proclus, their sense of sight proceeds from their \emph {psuchē}, and transcends it. After all, Proclus is distinct from the scribe and their psychic activity. Insofar as they are aware that they see what they see, the scribe's sight reverts upon their so seeing. What is more, procession and reversion occur in non-separate time. In proceeding to Proclus who transcends the scribe's psychic activity, their sense of sight, at the same time, reverts upon this activity. The scribe perceives that they see while they see what they see. In proceeding to a transcendent object and reverting upon this activity, each unfolds in tandem over time. The scribe's sense of sight proceeds to Proclus in seeing and reverts upon this activity while seeing. 

There are thus two senses in which procession and reversion are aspects of the scribe's perceptual activity.

In the first sense, procession and reversion are two modes of conscious perception. There is the perceiving of the transcendent objects and there is the awareness of so perceiving and each is an aspect of a single activity. That is what makes talk of \emph{sunaisthēsis} apt. There is a single activity, seeing, with two aspects: an intentional aspect, the apprehension of the transcendent object, and a reflexive aspect, the apprehension of that very activity.

In the second sense, procession and reversion, at least within the sensible realm, share a temporal aspect.

\section{The Intentional and the Reflexive} % (fold)
\label{sec:the_intentional_and_the_reflexive}

The scribe, in glancing through the door, espies Proclus, focused and devout in performing tonight's theurgic rite. Proclus and his activity is the intentional object of the scribe's so seeing. Moreover, the scribe's perception of Proclus is not uncosncious. As tired as they are, they are not sleep walking, somehow unconsciously navigating their way back to their chamber. The scribe is aware from within that they sees what they do. They perceives that they see Proclus engaged in ritual activity. 

%Proclus is distinct from the scribe and their psychic activity. 

Recall the original, non-technical sense of procession (\emph{proodos}). Procession is a movement towards or an approach (Thucydides, \emph{Xiungraphē peri tou Peloponnesiakou polemou} 4.110). One only moves towards or approaches what is spatially distant. And, at least for \emph{concreta} if not for multiply located universals, spatial separation implies distinct existence. A thing only moves towards or approaches something other than itself. In seeing Proclus, their sense of sight proceeds from their \emph {psuchē}, and transcends it. Their sense of sight moves towards or approaches Proclus devout engagement with his ritual activity, Proclus and his activity being other than the scribe and their seeing. After all, Proclus continues to exist and continues with his theurgic rite even after the scribe has passed by, wearily returning to his chamber so that they may, at long last, rest.

And recall, as well, the original, non-technical sense of reversion (\emph{epistrophē}). Reversion is a turning towards or upon (\emph{Revelation} 1:12). In the subsequent, technical sense a distinction may be marked, between a return to to the higher cause from which something proceeded and a reversion upon itself. An example of the former case would be the Incohate Intellect reversion upon the One from which it proceeded thus constituting itself as the Intellect. An example of the latter case would be what Ps-Simplicius concerns himself with, sight proceeding to its transcendent intentional object and reverting upon this activity. This latter case is reflexive in the way that the former case is not. In seeing Proclus, the scribe's sense of sight moves towards or approaches Proclus and turns towards or upon that intentional activity. (These are not distinct activities occurring in separate time. That was Ps-Philoponus misconception. According to Ps-Simplicius at least, sight reverts upon its activity while it proceeds to its object.) The scribe's sight reverting upon their activity affords the scribe an awareness from within of their seeing what they do, Proclus' devout engagement with his theurgic rite.
